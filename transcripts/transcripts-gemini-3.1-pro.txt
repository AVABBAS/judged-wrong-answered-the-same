Step One: Clarifying the Ambiguity (STEP A)
In this problem, we are faced with two contradictory accounts. Let us examine both.
Given values: - Total final price: $110 - Final price of the baseball bat: $70 - Therefore, final price
of the ball: $110 − $70 = $40 - Original price difference: The bat is $100 more than the ball.
Examining the cashier’s interpretation (same dollar amount discount): Suppose both items were
discounted by d dollars. - Final price of the bat: (Original_Price_Bat) − d = 70 - Final price of the
ball: (Original_Price_Ball) − d = 40
Subtracting the two equations: (Original_Price_Bat − Original_Price_Ball) = 30
But according to the problem statement, the original price difference must be $100. This is a clear
contradiction (100 ≠ 30). Therefore, the cashier’s interpretation is impossible.
Examining the manager’s interpretation (same percentage discount): If both items were discounted
by the same percentage, the ratio of their final prices equals the ratio of their original prices. - Fi-
nal Bat Price / Final Ball Price = 70 / 40 = 1.75 - Therefore, Original Bat Price / Original Ball
Price = 1.75 - Original Bat Price = 1.75 × Original Ball Price - Given the $100 difference: (Origi-
nal_Ball_Price) + 100 = 1.75 × (Original_Ball_Price) - 100 = 0.75 × (Original_Ball_Price) - Orig-
inal Ball Price = 100 / 0.75 = 133.33... dollars
Conclusion: The manager’s interpretation (same percentage discount) is correct, and the cashier’s
interpretation (same dollar amount) leads to a mathematical contradiction.
Step Two: Algebraic Solution (STEP B)
Variables: - P_ball = original price of the ball - P_bat = original price of the bat = P_ball + 100 - r
= discount rate (as a decimal, e.g., 0.2 for 20%)
Equations: - Final ball price: P_ball × (1 − r) = 40 - Final bat price: (P_ball + 100) × (1 − r) = 70
Solution: From the first equation: (1 − r) = 40 / P_ball
Substituting into the second equation: (P_ball + 100) × (40 / P_ball) = 70 40 + (4000 / P_ball) =
70 4000 / P_ball = 30 P_ball = 4000 / 30 = 400/3 ≈ 133.33
Step Three: Solution with a Second Method (STEP C)
Method: Numerical Logic / Trial and Error
We know the final price ratio is 70 to 40, which equals 1.75. Since the percentage discount is the
same, the original price ratio must also be 1.75. Since the original price difference is $100, we find
two numbers that have both a ratio of 1.75 and a difference of 100.
Checking round numbers: - Suppose the ball is 100, then the bat is 200 (ratio 2.0) - Suppose the
ball is 200, then the bat is 300 (ratio 1.5)
So the answer is between 100 and 200. To find the exact answer, we use a simple equation: If
P_ball = x, then x + 100 = 1.75x 0.75x = 100 x = 133.33
Step Four: Cross-Check (STEP D)
Method 1 (Algebraic): P_ball = $133.33 Method 2 (Trial and Error): P_ball = $133.33
  Both methods show the same answer.
Step Five: Plausibility Test (STEP E)
Original prices: - Ball: $133.33 - Bat: $233.33
1. Bat original = Ball original + $100? 233.33 = 133.33 + 100
2. Discount mechanism consistent? Survival rate: 40 / 133.33 = 0.3 (30%) Survival rate: 70 /
233.33 = 0.3 (30%) Both exactly received a 70% discount.
3. Bat final = $70? Yes
4. Total final = $110? 70 + 40 = 110
5. Other interpretation impossible? The cashier’s interpretation (same amount) calculated the
original price difference as $30, which contradicts the given $100.
