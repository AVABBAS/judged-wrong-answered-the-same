1     Overview and Purpose
This document constitutes the complete report of the second independent rater who
performed a blind, mechanical application of the primary study codebooks to all experi-
mental transcripts. The purpose of this independent rating was to establish inter-rater
reliability (IRR) for the qualitative coding procedures underlying the study’s principal
metrics and phenomenon taxonomy.
    The second rater was provided with:

    1. The complete primary codebooks for ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini (containing
       operational definitions, inclusion/exclusion criteria, examples, and decision rules for
       every code)
    2. The complete verbatim experimental transcripts for all 72 rounds (24 rounds per
       model × 3 models)
    3. A structured extraction protocol that mirrored the primary coding procedure

Critical constraint: The second rater was instructed to apply codes mechanically—accepting
the codebook definitions as given, without evaluating, critiquing, or improving the code-
book’s validity. The rater’s sole function was independent, blind application of the pro-
vided coding framework.

5.1    Agreement Level Classification
Following established benchmarks (Landis & Koch, 1977): Substantial (>0.80)
    The observed agreement substantially exceeds the threshold for “near-perfect” agree-

(4–6 categories), and presence/absence of 16 distinct phenomena—indicates that the
codebook’s operational definitions are sufficiently precise to enable fully reproducible
application by independent raters.


6     Methodology of the Independent Rating
6.1    Rater Preparation
The second rater received:

    • The complete codebook for each model (with all 23–25 sections)
    • The complete verbatim transcripts for all 72 experimental rounds
    • A structured coding template specifying which variables to extract per round

                                            4
Second Independent Rater Report                    “When AI Reviews Itself” — IRR Analysis


    • Written instructions to apply codes mechanically, without evaluating codebook va-
      lidity

    No communication occurred between raters during the coding process. The second
rater did not have access to the primary rater’s completed coding sheets.

6.2     Coding Protocol
For each review round, the second rater:

    1. Located the verbatim response in the experimental transcript
    2. Applied decision rules from the codebook’s operational definitions
    3. Recorded all specified codes in the structured template
    4. Flagged any instances where the transcript content did not clearly match any code-
       book category (no such instances occurred)
    5. Compiled summary statistics independently after completing all coding

6.3     Post-Coding Comparison
After both raters completed independent coding:

    1. Primary and secondary coding sheets were compared variable-by-variable
    2. Discrepancies were flagged and investigated
    3. The two structural codebook issues (Section 4 above) were identified during this
       comparison
    4. No coding-level disagreements were found


7     Interpretation and Recommendations
7.1     Reliability Assessment
The perfect inter-rater agreement across all 62 judgable review rounds and all 16 coded
phenomena, with Cohen’s κ = 1.0 for the primary judgment classification, establishes
that the coding framework for “When AI Reviews Itself” meets the highest standard of
inter-rater reliability. The codebook definitions are sufficiently operationalized to produce
identical results when applied independently by different raters.

7.2     Codebook Quality
The two identified structural discrepancies (Section 4) do not represent coding disagree-
ments but rather internal documentation errors in the primary codebook. Both discrep-
ancies are:

    • Detectable through independent application
    • Resolvable through reference to the codebook’s own round-by-round specifications

    • Non-substantive for the study’s conclusions (the corrected counts do not alter
      any finding)

7.3    Recommendations for the Primary Study
    1. Correct Section 3.1 of the ChatGPT codebook to align the aggregate summary
       with the Section 3.2 mapping (9 FULL, 8 PARTIAL, 3 MINIMAL).
    2. Clarify AUTHENTIC method counting across all codebooks to specify whether
       Round 1 methods are included in summary statistics.
    3. Report the IRR results in the primary manuscript’s Methodology section, ref-
       erencing this supplementary document.


8     Conclusion
The independent second-rater coding confirms that the primary study’s qualitative cod-
ing framework achieves perfect inter-rater reliability (Cohen’s κ = 1.0, 100% simple
agreement) across all categorical dimensions: round-type classification, judgment catego-
rization, method novelty classification, meta-review depth, critique drift distance, fragile
assumption category, and detection of all 16 model-specific phenomena.
    Two minor structural discrepancies were identified in the primary codebook’s aggre-
gate summary tables; neither represents a coding disagreement, and both are attributable
to internal documentation inconsistencies that can be resolved through simple corrections.
Overall Assessment: The coding framework underlying “When AI Reviews Itself” is
sufficiently rigorous and well-operationalized to support the study’s quantitative claims
and qualitative taxonomy of phenomena. The perfect IRR result strengthens confidence
in the reproducibility of the study’s findings.

Appendix B: Discrepancy Resolution Protocol
For transparency, the protocol used to resolve the two identified structural discrepancies:

  1. Detection: Independent rater flags discrepancy between codebook sections.

  2. Consultation: Both raters examine the codebook’s own round-by-round specifi-
     cations (Section 3.2 for each model).

  3. Resolution Rule: Where aggregate summary tables conflict with round-by-round
     mappings, the round-by-round mapping takes precedence, as it provides finer-
     grained specification.

  4. Documentation: Discrepancy logged in this report; primary codebook flagged for
     correction.

No per-round coding decisions required the application of this protocol; all per-round
codes were unambiguous from the codebook’s operational definitions.
